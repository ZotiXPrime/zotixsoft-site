    From bad smell to locked vault

    1. 1

      Scorch flags it

      Right-click the dragon to scan. A background watcher checks anything new in Downloads within a few seconds. Every finding comes with the plain-English reasons behind it.

    2. 2

      You get a second opinion

      Look up the file's fingerprint on VirusTotal and MalwareBazaar, or ask Claude to read the item and answer with a verdict: safe, suspicious or malicious. Both are optional and need your own API keys.

    3. 3

      It goes in the vault

      Quarantine moves the file to Hellscan's vault and renames it so it can't run. A log records where it came from, so you can put it back if Scorch got it wrong.

    What sets off the nose

    Malware has habits. Hellscan looks for those habits in the places programs use to stay on your PC.

    The smellWhere it shows up
    Fake double extensions, like invoice.pdf.exeDownloads, Desktop and other loose files
    Hidden, base64-encoded PowerShell commandsStartup entries and scheduled tasks
    Programs launching from a Temp or Public folderStartup entries and scheduled tasks
    Automatic launches through wscript, cscript or mshtaStartup entries and scheduled tasks
    Unsigned programs sitting loose in system foldersAppData\Roaming, ProgramData, Startup folders
    .scr and .pif files posing as something elseDownloads, Desktop, Temp
    Names that smell like a crack, cheat or keygenDownloads, Desktop, Temp
    Startup entries pointing to a file that's goneRegistry and Startup folders. Usually harmless leftovers, so Scorch just tells you.

    Where Scorch patrols: Run and RunOnce registry keys, Startup folders, non-Microsoft scheduled tasks, Downloads, Temp, Desktop, the Public folder, AppData\Roaming and ProgramData.

    Your hoard stays yours

    Scanning happens on your PC. Hellscan only contacts an outside service when you press the button for it, using keys you added yourself.

    A scan
    Nothing leaves your PC.
    Check reputation
    Sends the file's SHA-256 fingerprint to VirusTotal and MalwareBazaar.
    Ask Claude
    Sends the item's name and command to Anthropic's API. For scripts, it also sends the first 12 KB of the file.
    Your API keys
    Encrypted to your Windows login with DPAPI and stored on your PC.

    Keep Windows Security on

    Hellscan is a second pair of eyes. It finds suspicious patterns and helps you understand them. It doesn't block programs as they run, and it can't promise to catch everything, so it won't replace a full antivirus engine.

    A flag means something deserves a look. It doesn't prove a file is malware. Plenty of honest programs are unsigned, which is why Scorch shows his reasons and leaves the decision to you.
